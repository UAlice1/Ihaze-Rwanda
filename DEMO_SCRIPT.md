## 🎥 RECORDING TIPS

### Vocal Delivery:
- **Pace:** Slightly slower than normal conversation
- **Energy:** Enthusiastic but professional (like TED talk)
- **Emphasis:** Stress key numbers (1,834, 72%, 16,057)
- **Pauses:** Brief pause after major points for emphasis
- **Clarity:** Enunciate clearly; imagine explaining to non-technical audience

### Visual Pacing:
- **Don't rush clicks:** Let interface load completely
- **Highlight with cursor:** Circle important numbers with mouse
- **Smooth transitions:** Don't jump between screens too fast
- **Show, don't just tell:** When you say "filter," actually click the filter

### Common Mistakes to Avoid:
- ❌ Saying "um," "uh," "like" (pause silently instead)
- ❌ Apologizing ("sorry, let me just...") 
- ❌ Reading the script in monotone
- ❌ Going over 3:00 minutes (judges will cut you off)
- ❌ Technical errors (test EVERYTHING beforehand!)
- ❌ Forgetting to show URLs/contact info at the end

## 💬 OPTIONAL: Q&A PREPARATION

After your demo, judges may ask questions. Prepare for:

### Expected Questions:

**Q: "Your data is from 2016-2017. Isn't that too old?"**
**A:** "Great question. We used 2016-2017 data to build and validate the methodology. The framework is designed to work with any NISR agricultural survey. We'd update with 2024 data before operational deployment. The analytical approach—finding overlaps of need, barriers, and service gaps—remains relevant regardless of data year."

---

**Q: "How do you handle data privacy?"**
**A:** "We only expose aggregate district-level statistics publicly. Individual household microdata never leaves the backend and is not in our GitHub repository. The public dashboard shows counts and percentages only, fully compliant with NISR data use terms."

---

**Q: "What makes this better than a simple Excel dashboard?"**
**A:** "Three things: First, we do the complex data validation and joins automatically—extension officers don't need statistical expertise. Second, our barrier-specific filtering helps match households to appropriate interventions. Third, the capacity planner helps with resource allocation—'If I have 50 officers, where should they go?' That's operational decision support, not just data display."

---

**Q: "Can this scale beyond Rwanda?"**
**A:** "The framework is scalable to any country with agricultural survey data. The specific indicators would need to be adapted to local contexts and datasets, but the core approach—evidence-based targeting of agricultural support—is universal."

---

**Q: "Why not use machine learning to predict future erosion?"**
**A:** "Excellent question. We chose evidence-based reporting over prediction for three reasons: First, we don't have time-series data to train predictive models. Second, agricultural officers trust *'Here's who reported problems'* more than *'Here's who we predict will have problems.'* Third, current needs are actionable now—prediction adds latency. That said, the platform could incorporate ML forecasting as a future enhancement if validated training data becomes available."
